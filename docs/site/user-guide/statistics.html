Understanding Royal Craps Statistics

Royal Craps records a large number of statistics while you play. This section describes what is recorded, how each statistic is counted, and the nuances that matter when you interpret the numbers. It assumes you already know the rules of craps and the standard bets.

Statistics are only as useful as your understanding of what they count. Several of the statistics in Royal Craps deliberately differ from what a casual reader would assume, for example a Hardway bet’s loss is split into easy way and seven, and a Place bet is not counted on the come out roll. Those choices are called out below.

Note

Craps dice are independent. Every statistic here describes the past and none of them predicts the next roll. Streaks, repeaters and “hot” or “cold” tables are interesting to study, but a number is no more likely to appear after a long absence than after a recent hit.

Where statistics are kept

Every statistic belongs to one of two owners and covers one of two time spans.

Table statistics describe the dice and the table: every roll made at the table, regardless of who bet. They are independent of which players are seated.

Player statistics describe one player: the bets that player made, the results of those bets, and that player’s bank activity. Each player has their own set, so you can compare how two players fared on the same rolls.

Current session statistics cover the time since you started the program. All-time statistics accumulate across every session. When a session ends, the current session statistics are merged into the all-time statistics and saved to the table and player YAML files. Because all-time values are kept in those files, they survive restarts. Current session values start over at zero each time you launch the game.

Note

The all-time maximum values (largest single win, longest streak and so on) are the maximum over all sessions, not the sum of the session maximums.

Roll counters and streaks

Most dice statistics are kept as a counter. A counter records two things:

  • the count, which is how many times the event occurred, and

  • the max repeats, which is the longest run of consecutive occurrences of that same event.

A run is consecutive rolls, not consecutive visits. If the event is “a 6 is rolled”, then a run of three means the dice came up 6 on three rolls in a row. The first occurrence starts a run and does not count as a repeat, so a number rolled twice in a row has one repeat.

Dice number counts

For each total from 2 to 12 the game counts how many times it was rolled and its longest back-to-back run. These are the basic statistics from which most players judge whether the dice are behaving as expected. Over a large number of rolls the counts approach the standard two-dice frequencies (a 7 on 1 roll in 6, a 6 or 8 on 5 rolls in 36 each, and so on).

Come out and point rolls

Every roll is either a come out roll (no point is established) or a point roll (a point is on). The game counts both, along with how many of each occurred back to back.

The game also counts, for each total from 2 to 12, how often that total was rolled while a point was on. This matters for bets that are not working on the come out roll, such as Place, Buy and Lay bets. Those bets can only be decided by point rolls, so these counts are the right basis for judging them.

Points and seven-outs

The number of points established is counted overall and per point number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10). A seven-out is a 7 rolled while a point is on. Each seven-out ends a shooter’s turn, so the game keeps a shooter roll count: the number of rolls in the current shooter’s turn, which restarts at zero on a seven-out. A come out 7 is a natural and does not end the turn.

Note

A shooter’s roll count includes the come out roll that established the point and any come out rolls after a point is made. It is the length of the whole turn, not only the time with a point on.

Come out results

On a come out roll the game counts 2, 3, 7, 11 and 12 separately, and also counts craps as a group (2, 3 and 12). Each of these is a counter, so you can see, for example, the longest string of come out sevens.

The game also counts the come out decisions of the four line bets:

  • Pass Line wins on a come out 7 or 11 and loses on 2, 3 or 12.

  • Don’t Pass wins on 2 or 3 and loses on 7 or 11. A come out 12 is a push and is counted as neither a win nor a loss.

  • Come and Don’t Come are tracked the same way, using the roll made while a point is on as their come out roll.

Pass and Come points

For each point number the game counts how many times a Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass and Don’t Come bet on that point was won and how many times it was lost, with repeat streaks. This is what lets you see, for instance, how often a 4 is made compared with how often a 7 beats it. The ratio of wins to losses on a given point is expected to approach the true odds (2 to 1 for 4 and 10, 3 to 2 for 5 and 9, 6 to 5 for 6 and 8).

Field results

The Field is counted as a win on 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12 and a loss on 5, 6, 7 or 8, each with streaks.

Note

This is a count of rolls where a Field bet would have won or lost whether or not anyone bet. It does not account for the 2 and 12 payout (2 to 1 or 3 to 1 depending on table rules), which only affects the money statistics.

Hardway results

For 4, 6, 8 and 10 the game counts hard way wins (a pair rolled) and losses. Losses are split by cause:

  • easy way: the number was rolled any way other than as a pair, and

  • seven: a 7 was rolled.

This split is worth watching. A Hardway player who is losing mostly to sevens has a different problem from one losing mostly to easy ways, and the house edge you are really facing depends on which of the two dominates.

Note

Hardway statistics assume hardways work on the come out roll. When you are looking at the table statistics, Hardway counts therefore include come out rolls.

Bet statistics

Each player has bet statistics. These record every bet that reached a decision: win, lose, or neither (a bet that was taken down or that stayed up and was kept).

For every bet type the game records the count of wins and losses, the amount won or lost, and the amount bet. You can compare those to see profit or loss, the average bet, and how a bet type performed overall.

Overall totals are also kept: bets made, won, lost and kept; total dollars at risk, won, lost and kept; and the largest single bet, win, loss and keep. For the number of bets and the amount bet on one roll, the game tracks the current value, the maximum, and the total, which lets you see how heavily a player bets on a typical roll as well as on their heaviest roll.

Each bet type also records the distance, which is how many rolls its bets stayed up before they were decided. The average distance is the total distance divided by the count.

Contract amount and odds amount

Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass and Don’t Come bets can have odds. The amount won, lost and bet is stored as a combined figure, and the odds part is also stored separately. So you can see the profit on the line bet itself and the profit on the odds.

Note

Odds are not a separate bet type in the statistics. They appear inside their parent bet. The “amount bet” for a Pass Line bet includes any odds behind it, which is why the Pass Line’s dollars bet look larger than the contract bets alone.

When judging odds, look at the odds portion. It is the only part of the wager with a zero house edge, and it is the only part that tells you whether odds were worth taking.

Statistics by point and by roll

Bets are recorded by name and, where it matters, by the number that decided them:

  • Come out versus point: line bet decisions made on the come out roll are stored separately from decisions made after a point is established, so you can see how much of a Pass Line player’s profit comes from the come out and how much from the points.

  • By point: Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass, Don’t Come, Place, Buy, Lay and Hardway statistics are stored per number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10), so you can see which numbers made or lost money.

  • Do and Don’t side: the game can combine all “do” bets (Pass Line and Come) and all “don’t” bets (Don’t Pass and Don’t Come) into a single figure each, to show how the right-way and wrong-way sides compare.

  • Field by number: Field results are stored per rolled number, so you can see how much of the profit came from 2 and 12 versus the other winners.

One roll bets

Any Craps, Any Seven, Any 7/11, C&E, Horn and World are decided on the next roll. In addition to their totals, these are stored by the number that was rolled. For example, you can see how many times a Horn bet won on a 12 versus a 3.

Horn, C&E and World are combination bets. Part of the wager wins and the rest loses, so a single result may be a net win even though some of the components lost. The game classifies each decision:

  • Entire loss: the whole wager lost, for example a World bet on a point number.

  • Partial win: a net win with losing components, for example a Horn bet that hits a 2 or a C&E bet on 11.

  • Break even: a component won but net profit is zero, for example a World bet on a 7.

  • Clean win: a win with no losing parts, as with Field, Any Seven and Any Craps.

Note

For combination bets, the amount won or lost is stored net of the losing components. A Horn bet of $4 total that wins on a 2 does not count as a win of $30 plus three losses of $1. It counts as the net figure. If you want to know how often a Horn hit, use the by-roll statistics.

Hardway bets

Hardway losses are stored split by cause, as in the table statistics above: the easy way versus a seven. This means you can see for each hardway number whether a player is being beaten by sevens or by the easy way.

Simulated (“would have”) statistics

Besides what a player actually bet, the game can show how often each bet would have won or lost given the rolls seen at the table. These are the simulated counts. They answer questions like “how often would a Place 6 have won if I had kept it up the whole time?”

They are derived from the table roll statistics rather than from any player’s bets, so they apply even for bets the player never made. They make the following assumptions, which you should keep in mind:

  • Place, Buy and Lay bets are counted from point rolls only, since those bets are off on the come out roll.

  • Hardway bets are counted on every roll, as if they work on the come out.

  • One roll bets are counted on every roll.

Note

Simulated counts describe the dice and are not a measurement of your own performance. If your actual win rate on a bet type is well away from the simulated rate, the difference comes from when and how you bet, not from luck at the table.

Roll activity

The game also tracks activity per roll: how many bets were up, won and lost on a single roll, and the busiest roll seen so far. The last roll statistics describe only the most recent roll: the dollars and bets on the table before the roll, and the dollars and bets won and lost on it.

Repeaters

A repeater statistic answers the question serious players ask most often: how many times does a number show up before a 7 ends the run?

Royal Craps records this as a histogram. For a given number, the game stores how many times the number was hit before a 7, and how often each of those hit counts happened. For example, for the number 6 you might see:

Hits before 7    Times
0                 61
1                 52
2                 40
3                 29
...

Note

The game stores hits, not repeats. A number that appeared once before the 7 has one hit and zero repeats. Players often say “it repeated twice” to mean three hits. Make sure you convert when comparing against other sources.

Table repeaters

The table keeps two kinds, and the difference matters.

Point repeaters cover the numbers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12. Any 7 ends a run, including a come out 7. All rolls count, including the roll that establishes the point. This is the raw “hits before a 7” for each number, and is the statistic that matches how most players describe a number’s behaviour.

Place repeaters cover only 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10. Only rolls made while a point is on count as hits, and only a seven-out ends a run. A come out 7 does not end a Place run because a Place bet is not working on the come out roll. A run of zero hits is recorded when a seven-out ends a run with nothing in it, so the histogram’s zero entry reflects how often a Place bet on that number would have lost without ever being paid.

Note

A number rolled on the come out roll to establish the point is a hit for point repeaters, and is not a hit for place repeaters. That single difference accounts for most of the gap between the two histograms for the same number.

Player repeaters

Each player has their own repeaters, for the numbers 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 only. These are participation based. They measure the runs that the player’s own bets actually took part in, not every roll at the table.

  • Point repeaters count hits of a number while the player held a Pass Line or Come bet on that number. The roll that establishes the point counts as a hit, a number is counted at most once per roll, and any 7 ends the run.

  • Place repeaters count hits on the player’s winning Place bets. A seven-out ends the run.

  • Buy repeaters do the same for winning Buy bets.

Winning Place, Buy, Pass Line and Come bets are taken down in Royal Craps once paid, so a player who re-places a bet after a win continues the same run. A run therefore spans the re-placed bets rather than restarting at each bet.

The histogram’s zero entry has a precise meaning for players. A zero is recorded when the player held a Place or Buy bet on a number, a seven-out came, and the number was never hit. It is not recorded for numbers the player was not betting. If the number was hit but the bet was not up at the time, the run closes with the hits it did get.

Note

A come out 7 closes the runs only of Place and Buy bets that actually lost on that 7, that is, bets the player had working. It does not close runs on bets that are off.

Note

Running tallies of unfinished runs are not saved. If you quit in the middle of a run, that partial run is discarded rather than being added to the histogram.

Bank statistics

Each player keeps a record of bank activity:

  • the number and total amount of deposits,

  • the number and total amount of withdrawals,

  • the number and total amount of refills, and

  • the largest total deposited and the largest total withdrawn in a single session, each with the date it happened.

Note

A refill is money the bank adds automatically when a player’s balance falls below the configured refill threshold. It is tracked apart from deposits so that your deposits show money you chose to put in, and your refills show how often the bank ran low.

Session history

The game keeps a summary for each past session: its date and duration, how many players took part, how many bets were made, and the total intake and payout. From these it reports the number of sessions, the date of the first session, the longest session, and the results of each session in order, oldest first.

Intake is the money bet and payout is the money returned to players, so intake - payout is the amount the table kept. A table that runs long enough shows the house edge in these figures.

Reading the numbers

A few cautions that apply to every statistic above.

Sample size. Craps results swing widely over short samples. A few hundred rolls is too few to say anything about a bet’s performance. Expect numbers to approach their theoretical values only after thousands of rolls, and dollar results are noisier still because of varying bet sizes.

Table versus player statistics. Table statistics describe the dice. Player statistics describe the dice and the player’s timing, bet sizing and choices. Always compare a player’s numbers against the table’s before drawing a conclusion about the player.

House edge. The house edge of a bet is the average loss per dollar resolved. The game’s bet dialogs list each bet’s edge, so compare your own results to them. Single-roll bets resolve every roll and quickly show their edge. Line bets resolve slowly and take far more bets to settle.

What is not counted. Pushes (such as Don’t Pass on a come out 12) are not wins and losses. Bets that are kept or taken down do not contribute to win or loss counts.
