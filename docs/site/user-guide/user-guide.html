Royal Craps User Guide

This is the User Guide for Royal Craps version 0.1.0. It contains information useful for operating this application.

Royal Craps is a craps game for the Linux terminal. It plays the way a casino table does, with the full layout, the real payouts and the house commission, and you can play it alone or with computer buddy players at the table. It offers these features:

  • Line, Odds, Come, Place, Buy, Lay, Field, Hardway, and Proposition Bets

  • Choice of tables: Las Vegas Strip and High Roller

  • Automatic Odds

  • Quick Bets

  • Bet Flags: keep bets up, take them down, or work them on the next roll

  • Undo

  • Play with Up to Five Computer Buddies

  • Several players, each with a bankroll and history of their own

  • On-Screen Dice Roll Animation

  • Dice Roll history

  • Dice Stats

  • Player Stats Current Session

  • Player Stats Across All Sessions

  • Table Stats Current Session

  • Table Stats All Sessions

  • Terminal Based UI (future GUI)

Introduction

This guide shows you how to play Royal Craps and how to get the most from it. It is for the person at the keyboard. It does not assume that you know the game of craps, but it does not teach the rules of craps either. It explains how Royal Craps works, what its menus and settings do, and what the numbers on the screen mean.

The guide is arranged by what you want to do:

  • Players and Tables cover who is playing and where.

  • Undo, QuickBets, AutoOdds and Bet Flags cover the ways to place, change and take back bets with fewer keystrokes.

  • Configuring Royal Craps covers the files the game keeps, and the settings you may change.

  • Statistics explains the records the game keeps of how you, the table and the dice are doing.

How the game is built is described in the design document.

Players

At Royal Craps you play as one player. Your player has a name, a bank, and statistics that are kept from one visit to the next. You can have as many players as you like, and you can play as any of them, one at a time.

Other players can sit at the table with you. These are buddy players: they come with the game, they bet on their own, and you can watch how they do. A buddy player is not a player you can become; buddies are described at the end of this section.

Everything in this section is in the Player Menu. Press C for the Control menu, then p.

Key

What it does

l

List Players shows the players you can play as, each with a short description. The one you are playing now is marked (you).

s

Switch to Player changes who you play as.

r

Rename Player changes the name of the player you are playing as.

c

Create New Player asks for a name and a short description, then makes a new player.

d

Delete Player deletes a player you are not playing as.

b

List Buddies shows the buddy players, with their descriptions, and which are at the table.

j

Buddy Joins Table seats a buddy player at the table.

k

Buddy Leaves Table sends a buddy player away from the table.

Choosing From a List

Players are listed with their descriptions, like this:

┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Your Players                               │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Nathan   (you) | Primary player            │
│ Howie          | Left-handed dice thrower  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ [. or esc or enter] Back                   │
└────────────────────────────────────────────┘

A description that is too long for the screen is cut short with three dots.

Where you pick a player from a list, use the up and down arrow keys and press Enter, or press the letter shown next to the name. Esc backs out without doing anything.

Your First Player

The first time you run Royal Craps you are playing as a player named unnamed. Use Rename Player to choose a name. A name can be up to 16 letters, digits, spaces and ordinary punctuation, and no two players can have the same name. Capital and small letters count as the same: you cannot have both Howie and howie.

Note

Renaming changes only the name. Your bank and statistics stay with you. The player’s file keeps its original name, for example unnamed.yaml, so it is still found by config.yaml and state.yaml, which refer to players by file name.

Creating and Switching

Create New Player asks for a name and then for a short description, such as Left-handed dice thrower. The description is optional: press Enter with nothing typed to skip it. A description can be up to 40 characters. The new player starts with a fresh bank of $10,000 and no statistics. It does not switch to the new player. Use Switch to Player when you are ready to play as them.

When you switch, the player you were playing leaves the table and the new player takes their seat. If the player you were playing has bets on the table you are asked first. Those bets are not lost: they are taken off the table and the money goes back to that player’s bank.

Note

Whenever a player leaves the table, for any reason, any bets they have on the table are returned to their bank. This is the same when you quit the game.

Deleting a Player

Delete Player asks you to confirm. You cannot delete the player you are playing as, so you always have at least one player. A deleted player’s file is not erased. It is moved into a trash folder inside the players folder in your data directory, and you can move it back by hand if you change your mind. Because the player is also removed from players.yaml, you would need to add that entry back as well.

Buddy Players

Buddy players sit at the table with you and make their own bets. Use Buddy Joins Table and Buddy Leaves Table to change who is there. A buddy who leaves has any bets returned and has their results saved. A table holds at most six players, counting you, so a buddy cannot join a full table.

Buddy players cannot be renamed, deleted, or played as. If you want a player you can control, use Create New Player.

If you would like the same buddies at the table every time you start, see startWithTheseBuddyPlayers in the section on configuring Royal Craps.

Tables

Royal Craps comes with more than one table. Each table has its own rules, its own bet limits and odds, and its own bank, and it keeps its own statistics from one visit to the next. You play at one table at a time.

Everything in this section is in the Table Menu. Press C for the Control menu, then t.

Key

What it does

l

List Tables shows every table with a short description. The one you are at is marked (current).

s

Switch to Table moves you to another table.

. or Esc

Back to the Control menu.

Switching Tables

Pick the table you want from the list. The table you are at is not on it.

You can switch only while betting is open, between rolls. While the dice are rolling the switch is refused with a message, and you can try again when the roll is over. A point does not carry over. The new table starts with a come-out roll and you are the shooter.

When you switch:

  • Every bet on the table you leave is taken down and the money goes back to the player who made it. If you have bets on the table, you are asked to confirm first.

  • The table you leave records the session, and its statistics and bank are saved.

  • Your undo history is cleared, because the bets it would undo are gone.

  • AutoOdds forgets the bets it had handled, and starts fresh at the new table.

  • The roll history and the title bar start over for the new table.

Note

Your player, with its bank and statistics, goes with you. Only the table changes.

Your Buddies

The buddy players at the table can go with you or not. The choice is yours, and you make it in the Configure menu, under Buddies Follow (press b):

Follow

The buddies go to the new table with you. This is the usual setting.

Stay

The buddies leave the game when you switch. Their bets are returned and their results are saved. You can seat them again at the new table with Buddy Joins Table.

Ask

You are asked each time you switch, if any buddy is at the table. Answer Yes for the buddies to come along, or No for them to leave. Esc cancels the switch.

The setting belongs to the player you are playing as and is kept with that player. Buddies who go along keep their seats in the same order.

After the switch, a message tells you how many buddies came along and how many left the game, and how many bets were returned.

Your Saved Bets and Other Tables

Your QuickBets, your Last Fill amounts (the amount you used last time for each kind of bet) and your AutoOdds settings belong to your player, not to a table. They go with you when you switch. Tables differ in their limits and odds, so something that worked at one table can be refused at another:

  • A bet amount below the new table’s minimum, or above its maximum, is refused, and the message gives the table’s limits. A QuickBets entry places the bets that fit and skips the ones that do not.

  • An odds amount larger than the new table allows is refused.

  • An AutoOdds setting given as a multiple, such as 3x, follows each table’s own odds limit. One given as a dollar amount does not.

Royal Craps does not change your saved bets for you. If you will play at the new table for a while, change your QuickBets and AutoOdds settings to suit it, and place your bets by hand once so that Last Fill follows. Keep in mind that they then replace what you had, and they are what you will have when you go back. If you are only visiting, place your bets by hand and leave your saved ones as they are.

Note

The new table’s limits are shown in the title bar, after the table name.

Where the Tables Come From

The tables come with the game, like the buddy players do. They are listed in tables.yaml in the tables folder, and each table has a file there, such as las-vegas-strip.yaml and las-vegas-high-roller.yaml. There is no way to make a table in the game in this version.

Note

A table file holds the table’s rules and its statistics. If you have played before this version, a new table file is added to your tables folder only if it is not already there. Your existing tables, and the statistics in them, are never replaced.

The game opens at the table you were at when you last quit.

Undoing Bets

Changed your mind? Press u on the Betting menu to take back the last change you made to your bets. Press u again to take back the one before that, and so on, until you run out of changes to take back.

Undo is on the Betting Menu. Press b for the Betting menu, then u. From the table screen you can also press u by itself.

What can be undone

Each of these is one step that u takes back:

  • Placing a bet. Undo takes the bet down and returns its money.

  • Changing a bet’s amount. Undo puts back the amount the bet had before.

  • Setting or changing odds. Undo puts back the previous odds, or takes the odds down if there were none.

  • Taking a bet down. Undo puts the bet back on the table, with its odds, and takes its money again.

  • Taking down all your removable bets at once, with the all choice of Remove Bets (x). All of them are put back together by a single undo.

Money always follows the bets. Undoing a bet you placed returns your money, and undoing a bet you took down takes it again. If you have since spent the money, so that it can no longer be put back, the undo is refused with a message and nothing is changed.

Undo puts a bet back the way it was, even where the rules would not let you make that change by hand. For example, you can take down a DontPass bet after the point is set, and undo will put it back.

What cannot be undone

  • Anything before the last roll. Rolling the dice decides your bets, so the list of things you can undo starts empty after every roll. Undo is only for the betting you have done since the dice last landed.

  • While the dice are rolling. Betting is closed then. Wait until betting reopens and the steps are still there.

  • Other players’ bets. Undo only takes back the bets of the player you are playing as. A buddy’s bets are never touched. Each player has their own list, so switching players and back does not lose your steps, as long as you do not roll in between.

  • Changing tables. Your list starts empty at a new table.

  • Redo. There is no way to undo an undo. Place the bet again.

  • Things that are not bets. Settings, such as Animation Speed, are not affected.

Note

If there is nothing to undo, you get a message that says so. If an undo is refused for another reason, you get a message with the reason, and the list of steps that you could undo is cleared, because your bets may no longer match it. Your bets and your money are left as they were.

Undo does not change your Last Fill amounts. That still remembers the last amount you entered, even if you undid that bet.

QuickBets

A QuickBets entry is a named bundle of bets that you can place with a few key presses. For example, an entry called Place Hi can hold a Place bet on the 8, 9 and 10, so that one pick puts all three on the table.

Applying a QuickBets entry

Press b for the Betting Menu, then q. A list shows your entries, each with its name and the bets it holds. Pick one with the arrow keys and Enter, or press the letter shown next to it, and the bets are placed. Esc leaves the list without placing anything.

You can apply an entry whenever betting is open.

What happens to each bet

  • One bet per spot. If you already have a bet on a spot that the entry covers, that bet is set to the entry’s amount. It is not added to. If the amount is already the same, nothing changes.

  • Bets that cannot be placed are skipped. Perhaps you do not have the money, or the table does not allow that amount. The other bets in the entry are still placed, and a message tells you which ones were skipped and why. When every bet is placed there is no message.

  • Line odds. An entry may hold odds on a Pass Line or Don’t Pass bet. The odds are placed after the bet they sit on. Odds can only be placed once the point is set, so on the come out roll they are skipped. Odds on Come and Don’t Come bets cannot be part of an entry.

  • Your Last Fill amounts are updated, just as if you had placed each bet yourself.

Undo

Applying an entry is a single step for Undo. Press u once and every bet that the entry placed or changed is put back the way it was. See Undoing Bets.

Saving a bet as a QuickBets entry

The easiest way to build an entry is while you are entering a bet. In the dialog where you type the amount, press q for QuickBets - Set or Add. The dialog shows the bet and the amount you have typed, for example Place bet 6 $24, and offers these choices:

  • 1 Set as new QuickBets entry asks for a name and makes a new entry that holds this one bet.

  • a, b, c and so on, one for each entry you already have, add the bet to that entry. If the entry already has a bet on that spot, its amount is changed to the amount you typed.

Esc leaves without saving. After a choice, a message says what was saved and you are back at the amount dialog, where you can still place the bet.

The bet is saved as it is: you do not have to place it first. Press q again with a different bet to keep building the entry.

Some rules:

  • An entry’s name can be up to 24 characters and must not match another entry’s name, whatever the capital letters.

  • You can have up to 20 entries, and an entry can hold up to 12 bets. If more entries than fit on the screen exist, the Up and Down keys show the rest.

  • Entries can hold odds only for Pass Line and Don’t Pass bets. For other odds bets the message says so.

Changing your QuickBets entries

Press c for the Control Menu, then c for Configure, then q for QuickBets. A list shows your entries. Pick one, then pick what to do with it:

  • Rename asks for a new name, starting with the old one.

  • Change an amount lists the bets in the entry. Pick one and type its new amount.

  • Remove a bet lists the bets in the entry. Pick one to take it out. An entry always keeps at least one bet, so to get rid of the last one, delete the entry.

  • Delete asks you to confirm and then deletes the whole entry.

A message tells you what changed, and you are back at the Configure menu. Esc leaves any list or question without changing anything.

New entries, and more bets for an entry, are made from the bet amount dialog as described above, since that is where the bet is already chosen.

Changes are saved with your player when you leave the game.

Writing entries in the player file

Entries can also be written in your player file, in the Prefs section, while Royal Craps is not running:

Prefs:
  QuickBets:
    - name: Place Hi
      bets:
        - { betName: Place, pivot: 8,  amount: 24 }
        - { betName: Place, pivot: 9,  amount: 20 }
        - { betName: Place, pivot: 10, amount: 20 }
    - name: Line with odds
      bets:
        - { betName: PassLine, amount: 10 }
        - { betName: PassLine, isOdds: true, amount: 30 }

betName is the name of the bet, as it appears in your player file’s bet list. pivot is the point or number for bets that have one, such as Place, Buy, Lay and Hardway bets, and may be left out for the others. isOdds is only for the odds on PassLine and DontPass, and may be left out.

Some limits keep the lists readable. You can have up to 20 entries and an entry can hold up to 12 bets. An entry with no name or no usable bets, or with more entries than the limit, is ignored when the game starts, and the reason is written to the log.

AutoOdds

AutoOdds puts odds behind your contract bets for you. You say what odds you want for each kind of bet and each point, and after each roll AutoOdds places them. The odds are your money, so AutoOdds is off until you turn it on.

What you can set

For each of Pass Line, Don’t Pass, Come and Don’t Come, and for each of the points 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, you can set either:

  • an amount, such as $50, which is placed as it is; or

  • a multiple, such as 5x, which is that many times the contract bet. A $10 Pass Line bet with 5x on the 6 gets $50 odds when 6 is the point.

A point with nothing set gets no automatic odds.

Configuring AutoOdds

Press C for Control, c for Configure and then a for AutoOdds. A list offers:

  • Turn on or Turn off. Turning it off keeps what you set.

  • List Bets. A notice lists what is set for each bet type.

  • Set AutoOdds Bet. Pick a bet type, pick a point or All points, and type an amount like 50 or $50, or a multiple like 3x. Typing 0 clears it.

  • 3-4-5 preset. Sets 3x for the 4 and 10, 4x for the 5 and 9, and 5x for the 6 and 8, for all four bet types.

  • Clear all. After you confirm, removes everything you set.

Setting AutoOdds while you enter odds

While you enter an odds bet, press a for AutoOdds - Set or Unset. AutoOdds for that bet type and point is set to exactly the amount you have typed, in dollars. If you have typed nothing or 0, it is unset. Pressing a does not place the bet. You can press Enter afterwards to place the odds you typed.

If AutoOdds is off, the notice reminds you to turn it on.

When it acts

After each roll, once the point and the Come bets have settled and betting is open again, AutoOdds looks at your contract bets that have a point and no odds. It places the amount you set for that bet type and point:

  • The table’s limits apply. The amount is rounded down to the table’s odds increment and is no more than the table allows. If it is less than one increment, the odds are skipped and a notice tells you why.

  • Each bet is tried once. If you take odds down by hand, or the table refuses them, AutoOdds leaves that bet alone from then on.

  • Not enough money skips the odds. They are not tried again.

  • One undo. Everything AutoOdds places after a roll is one step for Undo (u).

While AutoOdds is on, the bottom bar shows AutoOdds on. After it places odds it shows how many, such as AutoOdds: 2 placed, until the next roll.

AutoOdds works for the player you are playing as, and its settings are kept in that player’s file with the rest of the player’s settings.

Bet Flags: Turning Bets Off and On

A bet that is working can win or lose on the next roll. A bet that is not working is left alone on that roll: it stays on the table, and it neither wins nor loses. Bet Flags lets you say which of your bets work.

At a real table the dealer decides by the house rule: Place, Buy and Lay bets, and odds, are off on the come out roll, and Hardways are always on. Bet Flags lets you change that for a roll, or until you change it back.

Which bets can be flagged

  • Place, Buy, Lay and Hardway bets.

  • The odds on a Pass Line, Don’t Pass, Come or Don’t Come bet. The flag is for the odds only, never for the bet itself. If the odds are not working on the roll that decides the bet, the bet still wins or loses and the odds are returned to you.

Pass Line, Don’t Pass, Come and Don’t Come bets with no odds, the Field and the one roll bets cannot be flagged.

The three states

  • Table Default follows the house rule above. Every bet starts this way.

  • Working means the bet is on for every roll, the come out too.

  • Not-Working means the bet is off for every roll.

A Next Roll choice changes only the next roll, then goes away on its own. It leaves the state you set alone. For example, a Place bet that is Working and set to Not-Working Next Roll sits out one roll and is Working again after.

Setting flags

Press b for the Betting Menu, then g for Bet Flags. The menu offers only the kinds of bets you have:

  • i Individual Bet. A list shows each bet with its flags now. Pick one.

  • a All Bets

  • o Odds Bets

  • p Place Bets

  • b Buy Bets

  • l Lay Bets

  • h Hardway Bets

Each choice opens a list of five changes, for example for place bets:

  • a Set Place Bets Not-Working

  • b Set Place Bets Not-Working Next Roll

  • c Set Place Bets Working

  • d Set Place Bets Working Next Roll

  • e Set Place Bets To Table Default

Pick one with the arrow keys and Enter, or press its letter. . or Esc goes back one step. A notice then says what was done, for example Set Place bets to Working: 2 bets changed, 1 already set. If you have no bet that can take a flag, the menu says so instead.

Setting Working, Not-Working or Table Default also cancels a Next Roll choice you made earlier. Taking the odds down to $0 sets that bet back to Table Default.

Seeing the flags

The amounts in your bet area tell you:

  • An amount that is dim is not working on the next roll.

  • An amount that is underlined has a flag set by you, or a Next Roll choice.

So a Place bet that is Not-Working shows dim and underlined. A Place bet that is on its house rule shows dim during the come out and normal while a point is set. You can change flags whenever betting is open.

Undoing flag changes

A flag change is a single step for Undo, however many bets it changed. Press u once and each bet gets back the flags it had.

Flags are not saved between games. Bets start on Table Default.

Configuring Royal Craps

Royal Craps needs no configuration. The first time it runs it creates what it needs and puts you at a table, ready to play. This section is for when you want something different: always starting as a particular player, for example, or starting without the other players.

Everything you can set lives in one small text file named config.yaml.

Where the Files Are

Royal Craps keeps your files in your own home directory. The locations follow the usual Linux (XDG) conventions, and each can be moved by setting the environment variable shown.

Location

What is there

~/.config/royalcraps ($XDG_CONFIG_HOME)

config.yaml, your settings. This is the only file in this section that you edit.

~/.local/share/royalcraps ($XDG_DATA_HOME)

Your players and tables, with their bank balances and statistics. Royal Craps saves these for you.

~/.local/state/royalcraps ($XDG_STATE_HOME)

state.yaml, which remembers where you left off, and the log file royalcraps.log.

Note

Players and tables are changed by the game every time you play. Do not edit them by hand while the game is running, and make a copy first if you want to experiment.

The config.yaml File

When Royal Craps first runs it creates config.yaml for you. Every setting in it is commented out, so out of the box the file changes nothing. To use a setting, remove the # at the start of its line and change the value. The game reads the file when it starts, so quit and restart the game for a change to take effect.

Royal Craps never rewrites this file. Your edits and your comments are safe.

A few things to know about the file format, which is called YAML:

  • Indentation matters. Use spaces, never tabs, and keep each setting lined up under the heading it belongs to.

  • A line that begins with # is a comment and is ignored.

  • A list is written one item per line, each starting with a dash.

  • File names such as arthur.yaml are the names of the files in your players and tables directories. Quotes around them are optional.

If you make a mistake that stops the file from being read, the game tells you which file and why when it starts. To get a fresh copy, delete config.yaml and start the game again. It creates a new one, and your players and tables are not touched.

Choosing the Table and Players

By default Royal Craps starts where you last left off: the same table, you playing as the same player, and with the same other players seated. These four settings let you override that. Each one is independent of the others, and any you leave out are decided by where you last left off.

table:
  startWithTable: "las-vegas-strip.yaml"
  startWithUserPlayer: "nathan.yaml"
  startWithBuddyPlayers: true
  startWithTheseBuddyPlayers:
    - "arthur.yaml"
    - "paige.yaml"
startWithTable

Always start at this table.

startWithUserPlayer

Always start as this player, even if you quit last time as someone else.

startWithBuddyPlayers

true or false, and true if you leave it out. A buddy is any other player seated at your table. Set this to false to always start by yourself, no matter who sat with you last time and no matter what is listed in startWithTheseBuddyPlayers.

startWithTheseBuddyPlayers

Always start with these buddies, up to five. Leave this out to start with the buddies from your last game.

A few examples:

Always start as nathan, with whoever was at the table last time.

Set only startWithUserPlayer: "nathan.yaml".

Always start alone.

Set only startWithBuddyPlayers: false.

Always start with the same three players at the Las Vegas table.

Set startWithTable, startWithUserPlayer and startWithTheseBuddyPlayers.

How Royal Craps Remembers

When you quit, Royal Craps writes down your table, your player, your buddies, your dice animation speed and your roll history format in state.yaml. Next time, any setting you did not make in config.yaml is taken from there. Your own settings always take priority over it. If you quit with nobody else at the table, the next game starts that way too.

The very first time, with no state.yaml yet, you start at the Las Vegas Strip table as the player unnamed, with two buddies, arthur and paige.

state.yaml is only a memory and is always safe to delete. The next game then starts from your settings, or from the first-time defaults. You never need to edit it. Anything you want to keep belongs in config.yaml.

Note

The game is built to always start. If config.yaml names a table or player that does not exist, say because of a typing mistake or a player you removed, the game ignores that one name, writes a warning to the log file and falls back to the next choice (where you left off, then the defaults). Check the log if the game does not start as you expected.

Other Settings

These settings are in config.yaml too. They are shown commented out with their default values.

table: maxSessions

How many past sessions each table keeps in its history, for the statistics across all sessions. When there are more, the oldest are dropped. The default is 50.

log: debug and log: trace

true turns on more detailed messages in the log file. This is useful when something is wrong and you are asked for the log, and it makes the file grow quickly, so leave both false otherwise.

Note

The file also lists rollHistory: scroll, sound and player: maxSessions settings. These are reserved for features that are not in the game yet, and changing them has no effect in this version.

Dice Roll Animation Speed

The dice tumble across the table before the roll is shown. The bets are not settled until the dice land, so you never see a result before the roll. If you would rather not wait, you can speed the animation up or turn it off.

The speeds are:

off

No animation. The roll shows at once.

fast

Twice as fast as normal.

normal

The usual pace. This is the default.

slow

One and a half times slower than normal.

To change the speed while you play, open the Control menu, choose Configuration, then Animation Speed (press s). Pick a speed from the list. It takes effect on the next roll. The current speed is marked.

The speed you pick is remembered in state.yaml when you quit, so the next game starts at the same speed. To always start at a particular speed, no matter what you chose last time, put it in config.yaml:

animation:
  speed: fast

A speed in config.yaml is used at the start of every game, and you can still change it during a game with the menu. The command line option --animation-speed (see below) overrides config.yaml for one run.

Note

If the speed is not one of the four words above, the game writes a warning to the log file and uses normal. Unquoted off is also understood in config.yaml.

Roll History Format

The roll history under the title bar lists the most recent rolls, newest first. You can choose how each roll is written. The formats run from the most compact, which fits the most rolls, to the most detailed:

Format

Looks like

1

7 8 6 10

2

7,8,6,10

3

7(34) 8(44) 6(33) 10(65)

4

7(3,4) 8(4,4) 6(3,3) 10(6,5)

The numbers in parentheses are the two dice. Format 1 is the default.

To change the format while you play, open the Control menu, choose Configuration, then Format Roll History (press f). Press 1 to 4, or move to a line and press Enter. The current format is marked. The whole history is redrawn at once in the new format and keeps its colors for seven outs, pass line winners and points.

Like the animation speed, the format you pick is remembered in state.yaml when you quit. To always start with a particular format, put it in config.yaml:

rollHistory:
  format: 3

A format in config.yaml is used at the start of every game, and you can still change it during a game with the menu.

Overriding Settings Temporarily

Settings come from several places. When the same setting is given in more than one, the later one in this list wins:

  1. The program’s built-in defaults and the settings shipped with it.

  2. Your config.yaml.

  3. Environment variables.

  4. The command line.

The command line is the quickest way to try something once without editing any file. These options are available:

--debug and --trace

Turn on the detailed logging described above for this run only.

--animation-speed

Set the dice roll animation speed for this run: off, fast, normal or slow. Anything else stops the game with a message listing the choices.

--usr-config-dir, --usr-data-dir and --usr-state-dir

Use another directory in place of the config, data or state locations above. Pointing all three at a new, empty directory gives you a brand new game, exactly as on a first run, without touching your real players. This is a good way to try the game out safely.

--help

Lists every option.

The environment variable ROYALCRAPS_START_TABLE chooses the table for a run, taking priority over config.yaml.

Understanding Royal Craps Statistics

Royal Craps records a large number of statistics while you play. This section describes what is recorded, how each statistic is counted, and the nuances that matter when you interpret the numbers. It assumes you already know the rules of craps and the standard bets.

Statistics are only as useful as your understanding of what they count. Several of the statistics in Royal Craps deliberately differ from what a casual reader would assume, for example a Hardway bet’s loss is split into easy way and seven, and a Place bet is not counted on the come out roll. Those choices are called out below.

Note

Craps dice are independent. Every statistic here describes the past and none of them predicts the next roll. Streaks, repeaters and “hot” or “cold” tables are interesting to study, but a number is no more likely to appear after a long absence than after a recent hit.

Where statistics are kept

Every statistic belongs to one of two owners and covers one of two time spans.

Table statistics describe the dice and the table: every roll made at the table, regardless of who bet. They are independent of which players are seated.

Player statistics describe one player: the bets that player made, the results of those bets, and that player’s bank activity. Each player has their own set, so you can compare how two players fared on the same rolls.

Current session statistics cover the time since you started the program. All-time statistics accumulate across every session. When a session ends, the current session statistics are merged into the all-time statistics and saved to the table and player YAML files. Because all-time values are kept in those files, they survive restarts. Current session values start over at zero each time you launch the game.

Note

The all-time maximum values (largest single win, longest streak and so on) are the maximum over all sessions, not the sum of the session maximums.

Roll counters and streaks

Most dice statistics are kept as a counter. A counter records two things:

  • the count, which is how many times the event occurred, and

  • the max repeats, which is the longest run of consecutive occurrences of that same event.

A run is consecutive rolls, not consecutive visits. If the event is “a 6 is rolled”, then a run of three means the dice came up 6 on three rolls in a row. The first occurrence starts a run and does not count as a repeat, so a number rolled twice in a row has one repeat.

Dice number counts

For each total from 2 to 12 the game counts how many times it was rolled and its longest back-to-back run. These are the basic statistics from which most players judge whether the dice are behaving as expected. Over a large number of rolls the counts approach the standard two-dice frequencies (a 7 on 1 roll in 6, a 6 or 8 on 5 rolls in 36 each, and so on).

Come out and point rolls

Every roll is either a come out roll (no point is established) or a point roll (a point is on). The game counts both, along with how many of each occurred back to back.

The game also counts, for each total from 2 to 12, how often that total was rolled while a point was on. This matters for bets that are not working on the come out roll, such as Place, Buy and Lay bets. Those bets can only be decided by point rolls, so these counts are the right basis for judging them.

Points and seven-outs

The number of points established is counted overall and per point number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10). A seven-out is a 7 rolled while a point is on. Each seven-out ends a shooter’s turn, so the game keeps a shooter roll count: the number of rolls in the current shooter’s turn, which restarts at zero on a seven-out. A come out 7 is a natural and does not end the turn.

Note

A shooter’s roll count includes the come out roll that established the point and any come out rolls after a point is made. It is the length of the whole turn, not only the time with a point on.

Come out results

On a come out roll the game counts 2, 3, 7, 11 and 12 separately, and also counts craps as a group (2, 3 and 12). Each of these is a counter, so you can see, for example, the longest string of come out sevens.

The game also counts the come out decisions of the four line bets:

  • Pass Line wins on a come out 7 or 11 and loses on 2, 3 or 12.

  • Don’t Pass wins on 2 or 3 and loses on 7 or 11. A come out 12 is a push and is counted as neither a win nor a loss.

  • Come and Don’t Come are tracked the same way, using the roll made while a point is on as their come out roll.

Pass and Come points

For each point number the game counts how many times a Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass and Don’t Come bet on that point was won and how many times it was lost, with repeat streaks. This is what lets you see, for instance, how often a 4 is made compared with how often a 7 beats it. The ratio of wins to losses on a given point is expected to approach the true odds (2 to 1 for 4 and 10, 3 to 2 for 5 and 9, 6 to 5 for 6 and 8).

Field results

The Field is counted as a win on 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12 and a loss on 5, 6, 7 or 8, each with streaks.

Note

This is a count of rolls where a Field bet would have won or lost whether or not anyone bet. It does not account for the 2 and 12 payout (2 to 1 or 3 to 1 depending on table rules), which only affects the money statistics.

Hardway results

For 4, 6, 8 and 10 the game counts hard way wins (a pair rolled) and losses. Losses are split by cause:

  • easy way: the number was rolled any way other than as a pair, and

  • seven: a 7 was rolled.

This split is worth watching. A Hardway player who is losing mostly to sevens has a different problem from one losing mostly to easy ways, and the house edge you are really facing depends on which of the two dominates.

Note

Hardway statistics assume hardways work on the come out roll. When you are looking at the table statistics, Hardway counts therefore include come out rolls.

Bet statistics

Each player has bet statistics. These record every bet that reached a decision: win, lose, or neither (a bet that was taken down or that stayed up and was kept).

For every bet type the game records the count of wins and losses, the amount won or lost, and the amount bet. You can compare those to see profit or loss, the average bet, and how a bet type performed overall.

Overall totals are also kept: bets made, won, lost and kept; total dollars at risk, won, lost and kept; and the largest single bet, win, loss and keep. For the number of bets and the amount bet on one roll, the game tracks the current value, the maximum, and the total, which lets you see how heavily a player bets on a typical roll as well as on their heaviest roll.

Each bet type also records the distance, which is how many rolls its bets stayed up before they were decided. The average distance is the total distance divided by the count.

Contract amount and odds amount

Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass and Don’t Come bets can have odds. The amount won, lost and bet is stored as a combined figure, and the odds part is also stored separately. So you can see the profit on the line bet itself and the profit on the odds.

Note

Odds are not a separate bet type in the statistics. They appear inside their parent bet. The “amount bet” for a Pass Line bet includes any odds behind it, which is why the Pass Line’s dollars bet look larger than the contract bets alone.

When judging odds, look at the odds portion. It is the only part of the wager with a zero house edge, and it is the only part that tells you whether odds were worth taking.

Statistics by point and by roll

Bets are recorded by name and, where it matters, by the number that decided them:

  • Come out versus point: line bet decisions made on the come out roll are stored separately from decisions made after a point is established, so you can see how much of a Pass Line player’s profit comes from the come out and how much from the points.

  • By point: Pass Line, Come, Don’t Pass, Don’t Come, Place, Buy, Lay and Hardway statistics are stored per number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10), so you can see which numbers made or lost money.

  • Do and Don’t side: the game can combine all “do” bets (Pass Line and Come) and all “don’t” bets (Don’t Pass and Don’t Come) into a single figure each, to show how the right-way and wrong-way sides compare.

  • Field by number: Field results are stored per rolled number, so you can see how much of the profit came from 2 and 12 versus the other winners.

One roll bets

Any Craps, Any Seven, Any 7/11, C&E, Horn and World are decided on the next roll. In addition to their totals, these are stored by the number that was rolled. For example, you can see how many times a Horn bet won on a 12 versus a 3.

Horn, C&E and World are combination bets. Part of the wager wins and the rest loses, so a single result may be a net win even though some of the components lost. The game classifies each decision:

  • Entire loss: the whole wager lost, for example a World bet on a point number.

  • Partial win: a net win with losing components, for example a Horn bet that hits a 2 or a C&E bet on 11.

  • Break even: a component won but net profit is zero, for example a World bet on a 7.

  • Clean win: a win with no losing parts, as with Field, Any Seven and Any Craps.

Note

For combination bets, the amount won or lost is stored net of the losing components. A Horn bet of $4 total that wins on a 2 does not count as a win of $30 plus three losses of $1. It counts as the net figure. If you want to know how often a Horn hit, use the by-roll statistics.

Hardway bets

Hardway losses are stored split by cause, as in the table statistics above: the easy way versus a seven. This means you can see for each hardway number whether a player is being beaten by sevens or by the easy way.

Simulated (“would have”) statistics

Besides what a player actually bet, the game can show how often each bet would have won or lost given the rolls seen at the table. These are the simulated counts. They answer questions like “how often would a Place 6 have won if I had kept it up the whole time?”

They are derived from the table roll statistics rather than from any player’s bets, so they apply even for bets the player never made. They make the following assumptions, which you should keep in mind:

  • Place, Buy and Lay bets are counted from point rolls only, since those bets are off on the come out roll.

  • Hardway bets are counted on every roll, as if they work on the come out.

  • One roll bets are counted on every roll.

Note

Simulated counts describe the dice and are not a measurement of your own performance. If your actual win rate on a bet type is well away from the simulated rate, the difference comes from when and how you bet, not from luck at the table.

Roll activity

The game also tracks activity per roll: how many bets were up, won and lost on a single roll, and the busiest roll seen so far. The last roll statistics describe only the most recent roll: the dollars and bets on the table before the roll, and the dollars and bets won and lost on it.

Repeaters

A repeater statistic answers the question serious players ask most often: how many times does a number show up before a 7 ends the run?

Royal Craps records this as a histogram. For a given number, the game stores how many times the number was hit before a 7, and how often each of those hit counts happened. For example, for the number 6 you might see:

Hits before 7    Times
0                 61
1                 52
2                 40
3                 29
...

Note

The game stores hits, not repeats. A number that appeared once before the 7 has one hit and zero repeats. Players often say “it repeated twice” to mean three hits. Make sure you convert when comparing against other sources.

Table repeaters

The table keeps two kinds, and the difference matters.

Point repeaters cover the numbers 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12. Any 7 ends a run, including a come out 7. All rolls count, including the roll that establishes the point. This is the raw “hits before a 7” for each number, and is the statistic that matches how most players describe a number’s behaviour.

Place repeaters cover only 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10. Only rolls made while a point is on count as hits, and only a seven-out ends a run. A come out 7 does not end a Place run because a Place bet is not working on the come out roll. A run of zero hits is recorded when a seven-out ends a run with nothing in it, so the histogram’s zero entry reflects how often a Place bet on that number would have lost without ever being paid.

Note

A number rolled on the come out roll to establish the point is a hit for point repeaters, and is not a hit for place repeaters. That single difference accounts for most of the gap between the two histograms for the same number.

Player repeaters

Each player has their own repeaters, for the numbers 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 only. These are participation based. They measure the runs that the player’s own bets actually took part in, not every roll at the table.

  • Point repeaters count hits of a number while the player held a Pass Line or Come bet on that number. The roll that establishes the point counts as a hit, a number is counted at most once per roll, and any 7 ends the run.

  • Place repeaters count hits on the player’s winning Place bets. A seven-out ends the run.

  • Buy repeaters do the same for winning Buy bets.

Winning Place, Buy, Pass Line and Come bets are taken down in Royal Craps once paid, so a player who re-places a bet after a win continues the same run. A run therefore spans the re-placed bets rather than restarting at each bet.

The histogram’s zero entry has a precise meaning for players. A zero is recorded when the player held a Place or Buy bet on a number, a seven-out came, and the number was never hit. It is not recorded for numbers the player was not betting. If the number was hit but the bet was not up at the time, the run closes with the hits it did get.

Note

A come out 7 closes the runs only of Place and Buy bets that actually lost on that 7, that is, bets the player had working. It does not close runs on bets that are off.

Note

Running tallies of unfinished runs are not saved. If you quit in the middle of a run, that partial run is discarded rather than being added to the histogram.

Bank statistics

Each player keeps a record of bank activity:

  • the number and total amount of deposits,

  • the number and total amount of withdrawals,

  • the number and total amount of refills, and

  • the largest total deposited and the largest total withdrawn in a single session, each with the date it happened.

Note

A refill is money the bank adds automatically when a player’s balance falls below the configured refill threshold. It is tracked apart from deposits so that your deposits show money you chose to put in, and your refills show how often the bank ran low.

Session history

The game keeps a summary for each past session: its date and duration, how many players took part, how many bets were made, and the total intake and payout. From these it reports the number of sessions, the date of the first session, the longest session, and the results of each session in order, oldest first.

Intake is the money bet and payout is the money returned to players, so intake - payout is the amount the table kept. A table that runs long enough shows the house edge in these figures.

Reading the numbers

A few cautions that apply to every statistic above.

Sample size. Craps results swing widely over short samples. A few hundred rolls is too few to say anything about a bet’s performance. Expect numbers to approach their theoretical values only after thousands of rolls, and dollar results are noisier still because of varying bet sizes.

Table versus player statistics. Table statistics describe the dice. Player statistics describe the dice and the player’s timing, bet sizing and choices. Always compare a player’s numbers against the table’s before drawing a conclusion about the player.

House edge. The house edge of a bet is the average loss per dollar resolved. The game’s bet dialogs list each bet’s edge, so compare your own results to them. Single-roll bets resolve every roll and quickly show their edge. Line bets resolve slowly and take far more bets to settle.

What is not counted. Pushes (such as Don’t Pass on a come out 12) are not wins and losses. Bets that are kept or taken down do not contribute to win or loss counts.
